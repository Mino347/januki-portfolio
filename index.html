01 — About

Hello, I’m Januki De Silva

I am a third year Fashion Design student working at the meeting point of emotion, narrative, and material. My practice is concept-driven: every collection begins with a story or a feeling, and I build the garment around it through research, textile experimentation, and visual development.

I am drawn to the way clothing can carry meaning beyond the surface. Through manipulated fabrics, layered surfaces, and considered silhouettes, I turn abstract experiences into wearable form. Using fashion illustration, digital CAD, styling, and trend forecasting, I develop ideas from first sketch to finished concept.

My goal is to become a designer whose work challenges conventional ideas of beauty and gives people a way to express who they are.

Parasomnia

01 — Avant-garde

Parasomnia

Parasomnia treats nightmares and sleep disturbance as a psychological landscape. It is anchored by Fuseli’s The Nightmare (1781), where the incubus crouches on the sleeper’s chest. I wanted to show where fear and distress are felt in the body, not just illustrate a dark mood.

Three words guided every decision: distress (surface damage), messy (how the garment behaves on the body) and vivid (one sharp zone of focal intensity). I mapped eight emotional and bodily zones to eight repeatable silhouette archetypes. For the textile I bonded ripped thread, fabric and cord scraps together with water-soluble fusing to make a new layered fabric, then attached it into the denim and shirt base. The palette is locked to jet black, slate gray, mushroom beige and deep burgundy, so the collection reads as one dream.

Sustainability aim

The collection began with a rule of no store-bought fabric. Every textile came from discarded or thrifted material, including waste washed black denim and old long-sleeve shirts, with offcuts, threads, cords and scraps. The distressed look suits this approach, because tears, frays and damage become part of the design instead of flaws to hide.

Thrifted materials need care, so I researched safe cleaning methods and checked for toxins and skin-friendliness before using them. Waste stays in use at every scale, from whole garments down to the smallest scraps.

Sacred Skin

02 — Industry analysis

Sacred Skin

Approach: group project. We researched and analyzed Teejay Lanka PLC’s operations, market position and consumer behavior to find a gap in the industry. Teejay is a leading Sri Lankan knitted fabric manufacturer that supplies lingerie and activewear brands, but it hasn’t expanded into lace production or hybrid materials. That limits what partner brands can develop in categories like resort wear.

Our solution was a lace and knit resort wear collection for Intimissimi. It combines breathable, sustainable knits with refined lace textures, and it is designed for female, male and gender-fluid customers. The hybrid material approach supports product diversification and shows how Teejay could grow into new fashion markets as a creative textile partner.

Concept

Sacred Skin reimagines the sacred as something intimate and human. It starts from Orvieto Cathedral, with its striped limestone and basalt, shimmering mosaics, arches and ornament, and shifts spirituality from the distance of monumental architecture to the closeness of the body. The collection treats the body as a space of reverence, where softness, vulnerability and movement replace rigid structure.

Transparency, lightness and tactile layers carry this idea. The cathedral’s flowing lines and decorative motifs become airy silhouettes, delicate textures and lace detailing. The result is devotion that is lived and felt, resting gently on the skin, not observed from afar.

Cosmic Heirloom

03 — Global Crossroads

Cosmic Heirloom

Approach: urban contemporary wear. Global Crossroads brings two cultures together: Afrofuturism and Mexican Otomi embroidery. The Afrofuturist side supplies cosmic imagery and a vision of the future. The Otomi side, drawn from Tenango embroidery, supplies dense, colorful hand-stitched patterns of animals, birds and flowers. Together they form a collection where heritage is carried into a futuristic identity.

The collection is told through The Threadwalker’s Journey, a symbolic story of a futuristic traveler who moves through time and space, weaving ancestral wisdom into future vision. Each chapter is a phase of awakening, from reconnecting with cultural roots and hearing the echoes of the earth, to sending signals into space and embracing new identities. Garments evolve along the way and become vessels of memory, resistance and transformation.

Concept

Cosmic Heirloom is the idea of carrying ancestral wisdom and cultural heritage into the future. It preserves the past by transforming it into something new and visionary, not by keeping it in a museum. “Heirloom” stands for the traditions, stories and artistry passed down through generations. “Cosmic” suggests that this heritage doesn’t belong to one time or place, but crosses borders and eras into a futuristic, imaginative world. A Cosmic Heirloom becomes a symbol of resilience, pride and evolution, rooted in the past but created for the stars.

Gucci editorial styling

04 — Editorial styling

Gucci Editorial Styling

Approach: group project. For this project we researched Gucci’s brand campaigns and chose the Ancora campaign from Spring/Summer 2024. It was unveiled in January 2024, a few months after the runway show, and was Sabato De Sarno’s first advertising campaign as creative director, turning his runway vision into commercial imagery. The name plays on the Italian word’s double meaning of “again” and “still”, which Gucci framed as “also now, also then.”

From the campaign we selected six looks and styled them as they appear, matching the garments, accessories, proportions and attitude of the originals. The campaign’s saturated whites, deep burgundy, navy and light-wash denim against a clean studio backdrop, with models posed close together in a relaxed group, set the standard we worked to recreate. The project let us study how a luxury house builds a campaign look through color, silhouette, accessories and posing.

03 — Drawing

Illustration

I create expressive illustrations through both hand sketching and digital drawing. My hand sketches rely on layered linework and cross-hatching to build depth, texture, and mood, while my digital work in Clip Studio Paint explores soft shading, rich color, and detailed rendering. Across both approaches, I focus on capturing emotion, character, and individuality in every face, and I use illustration as the starting point for developing my fashion concepts.
